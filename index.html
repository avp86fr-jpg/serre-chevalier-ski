Screen & conditions

Screen viewBrowser view
Orientation

Follows your device rotation.

For an app view without the address bar, add Alpine to your Home Screen and open it there. On iPhone, use Share → Add to Home Screen.

Camera motion adds gentle banking and surface vibration; choose Off for a steady camera. Dynamic environment animates clouds, wind, branches and flags. Mountain sound adds wind and ski-edge feedback. On touchscreens, steer with your left thumb and hold brake or push/tuck with your right. You can hold both sides at once. Rotation pauses an active descent so you can settle into the new layout.
WELCOME TO ALPINE

A real place.
A first playable chapter.

Open world lets you choose a starting piste and steer freely across the mapped mountain. Continue through junctions, explore off-piste, and board a nearby lift while moving slowly. Use Map & travel (M) to visit any piste start or lift station; E boards or skips a lift ride. R returns to your chosen start. Buildings and modeled tree trunks stop your movement; turn away or relocate using the map. Other skiers are ambient traffic. Gates and Descent retain the original timed, assisted runs.

Choose a mapped piste and start a timed descent. Aim through the gates, carve with A / D or ← / →, push, then tuck with W / ↑ and brake with S / ↓ or Space. C switches camera, P or Escape pauses, R restarts, and F toggles fullscreen.

Gate challenge rewards clean, alternating turns with a line score and combos. A missed gate adds 2 seconds to your result. Free descent removes gate penalties. Your best result in each mode is saved locally, with a ghost to race next time. The browser keeps ghosts for up to eight recent route/mode combinations.

In open world, press J or Shift to pop off a lip or kicker. Hold W in the air for a grab; land cleanly to keep speed. Villeneuve Snowpark, Chantemerle Boardercross and Briançon Funnycross have built kickers beside the mapped pistes. Live weather is fetched from Open-Meteo at Villeneuve every time the game loads, then every 15 minutes; a saved observation is used only if the live request fails. Atmosphere can still be overridden in Settings. A standard gamepad is supported: left stick to carve, RT or A to push/tuck, LT or B to brake, X for camera, Y to restart and Start to pause. Adaptive graphics adjusts resolution to sustained frame load while preserving the terrain beneath your skis. Settings offers camera motion and dynamic clouds, gusts, spindrift, branches and flags. Groomed pistes use bright corduroy and grooming lanes; off-piste powder has cooler, irregular snow relief. A shaded snow shoulder and colored poles mark the transition. These edges follow estimated game widths, not surveyed resort boundaries. Weather transitions blend gradually. The skier has articulated knees and elbows, a deeper tuck, ski edging, a braking plough and pole strokes. First-person eye height follows the crouch. Performance mode uses a simpler skier mesh with the same animation rig. Mountain sound separates wind from ski-edge and braking noise. These conditions are simulated.

On a phone, hold and slide your left thumb to steer, with a separate right thumb for braking or pushing. Open Map & travel during exploration to choose another piste or lift. Tap Change piste in the mountain overview to open the route selector. The fullscreen button sits beside Settings. Choose Auto, Portrait or Landscape in Screen & conditions; browsers that cannot lock orientation will ask you to rotate your device. Focus view keeps the game uncluttered when native fullscreen is unavailable.

What’s modeled

Real elevation and mapped pistes cover the valley’s four main sectors.

Terrain uses IGN LiDAR HD ground elevations sampled every 4 m around the piste network and every 32 m across the valley. A 12 m border blend joins terrain detail levels. Nearby ground geometry and ski motion share the same height model; distant terrain uses less detail. Official names, grades and sectors are matched to candidate OpenStreetMap piste lines. IGN woodland boundaries guide procedural tree placement.

This remains a prototype. Piste extents need verification; disconnected sections are listed separately. Building wall outlines and orientations follow OSM footprints; heights, roofs, facade details, snow cover, tree species proportions, lift structures, gates and the skier are approximations. Winter larches and red glazed gondolas are modeled from photo references. Imagery is a reference, not a measured 3D reconstruction. Speed is measured from 3D travel in metres per second and displayed in km/h. Timing uses a fixed 120 Hz simulation; extended frame stalls pause the descent.

Reference pass: IGN aerial imagery and resort photography guide the village layout, winter woodland and lift appearance. Piste widths and full route extents still need on-site verification.
